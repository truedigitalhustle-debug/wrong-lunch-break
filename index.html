The method

Two clips. One reference for each thing on screen.

The girl, the creature, the place and the zombie each get their own picture. That is what keeps them the same from shot to shot. The video prompts then only have to say what happens.

Make the girl.One character sheet: full body on the left, close-up on the right. This is @Image1 .
Make the place.One empty photo of the location with no people in it. This is @Image3 .
Make the creature standing in that place.Feed the girl and the place into the image model as references and ask for her creature form standing there, in her clothes. This is @Image2 .
Make the zombie.One more character sheet. This is @Image4 .

What I learned the hard way

Five things that saved this video

The change has to happen to the body. My first version had a mouth open up across her like a zipper while she stood still facing the camera. It looked like a sticker laid over the picture. The version that worked has her spine, arms and skin change while she walks.
Move the camera. A locked-off, straight-on shot makes any effect look pasted on. Handheld, at an angle, following her, looks like a film crew was there.
Too much wet flesh gets blocked. A design with an open chest and bare skin was refused as unsafe. Dry grey leathery skin, with her apron still on, went straight through.
Say the clothes stay on. In one take her jeans vanished halfway through the change and came back in the next shot. I cut away just before it happened, and added a line to the prompt so it doesn't happen to you.
Check which side people come from. After I joined the clips, the zombie ran in from the wrong side. Mirroring that one shot fixed it for free.

Draft first, pay later

Every take on this page started as a low-resolution draft. I threw two of them away. Only render the full version of a take you have already watched and liked.
